how to find out what am i struggling with daily basis?

When you are constantly moving and executing tasks, it can be remarkably difficult to see what is actually slowing you down. The things you struggle with on a daily basis often become so routine that they just feel like "the way things are," rather than problems that can be solved.

Sometimes the best way to uncover hidden friction is to treat your daily routine like a complex system that needs debugging. Just like profiling an application to find bottlenecks, you can audit your day to find out where your energy and focus are leaking.

Here is a structured approach to identifying your daily struggles:

1. Maintain a "Friction Log"
For three days, keep a simple text file or notepad open next to your workspace. Every time you feel a spike of annoyance, lose your focus, or procrastinate, write it down.

Look for micro-frictions: Are you constantly losing your flow state because of context switching? Does a specific part of your workflow feel clunky or repetitive?

Note the feeling: Don't just write "meetings." Write "felt drained after the 10 AM sync because there was no clear agenda."

2. Audit Your Energy, Not Just Your Time
We often track our hours, but we rarely track our mental resources. Set an alarm to go off three times a day (e.g., 10 AM, 2 PM, 5 PM) and quickly rate your energy on a scale of 1 to 10.

When does your system crash?

What task were you doing right before the crash? Often, the things we struggle with most are the things that drain our cognitive battery the fastest, even if they don't take much time.

3. Analyze Your Workarounds
Look for the places in your life or workflow where you have built elaborate workarounds. When we struggle with the fundamental architecture or design of a system—whether it's our daily schedule, our digital workspace, or our task management—we tend to build messy patches over the problem instead of fixing the root cause. If you find yourself constantly fighting your own tools or schedule to get things done, you've found a core struggle.

4. The "5 Whys" for Daily Pain Points
Once you identify a surface-level struggle, dig down to the root cause. If your friction log says "struggled to start working this morning," ask why.

Why? Because I felt overwhelmed by my task list.

Why? Because I didn't prioritize it yesterday.

Why? Because I worked late and just wanted to log off.

Why? Because a project took longer than expected.

Why? Because I didn't clearly scope the requirements before coding.

Suddenly, a "morning motivation" struggle is revealed to actually be a project-scoping struggle.

To help narrow this down and start debugging: if you had a magic wand and could completely automate or eliminate just one specific, recurring annoyance from your current workflow, what would it be?

# Lightweight Development Plan: Mood & Energy Tracker Web Application
**Project Owner:** An Lê

## 1. Architecture & Tech Stack Overview
* **Language:** TypeScript (Strict mode enabled)
* **Frontend Framework:** React + Vite (Chosen specifically for a lightweight deployment. Vite compiles to highly optimized, static assets that deploy near-instantly on Vercel, completely avoiding the overhead of Server-Side Rendering).
* **Styling:** Tailwind CSS (Clean, minimalist UI with high-contrast elements)
* **Backend, Database & Auth:** Supabase 
* **Deployment:** Vercel (Static SPA deployment pipeline)

## 2. Authentication & Security (Supabase Auth)
Authentication is a core requirement and will be handled entirely via Supabase's native auth module.
* **Login Methods:** Standard Email/Password login UI, with the option to easily toggle on OAuth (e.g., Google or GitHub) in the Supabase dashboard.
* **Protected Routes:** The React router will utilize an "Auth Guard" component. If a user is not authenticated, they are automatically redirected to the `/login` screen.
* **Session Management:** Utilize `@supabase/supabase-js` to handle session persistence in local storage and automatically attach the user's JWT to all backend requests.
* **Row Level Security (RLS):** Database policies on the `daily_logs` table will strictly enforce `user_id = auth.uid()`, ensuring the database physically prevents users from accessing anyone else's data, regardless of the API call made.

## 3. Environment Strategy (Development vs. Production)
* **Supabase:**
  * `mood-tracker-prod`: Production database and Auth configuration.
  * `mood-tracker-dev`: Development/Staging environment.
* **Vercel Setup:**
  * Configure environment variables (`VITE_SUPABASE_URL` and `VITE_SUPABASE_ANON_KEY`). 
  * Map Vercel Production deployments to the `mood-tracker-prod` credentials.
  * Map Vercel Preview/Dev deployments to `mood-tracker-dev` credentials to ensure testing never corrupts live data.

## 4. Database Schema
* **`users`**: Auto-managed internally by Supabase Auth.
* **`daily_logs`**:
    * `id` (uuid, primary key)
    * `user_id` (uuid, foreign key to auth.users)
    * `log_date` (date)
    * `mood_score` (int2, range 1-5)
    * `energy_level` (int2, range 1-10)
    * `tags` (text array) - *e.g., ['coding', 'workout', 'poor-sleep']*
    * `created_at` (timestamptz)

## 5. User Interface & Experience
* **Frictionless Keyboard Entry:** Implement Vim-style navigation bindings. Users should be able to use `j`/`k` to navigate vertically between the mood and energy inputs, and `h`/`l` to adjust the sliding scales left or right. This guarantees lightning-fast daily logging without ever reaching for a mouse.
* **Aesthetic Direction:** A high-contrast, minimalist, almost retro visual style. Focus heavily on pure utility, fast load times, and data clarity over heavy animations.
* **Core Views:**
    1. **Login/Signup:** A fast, lightweight authentication screen.
    2. **Dashboard / Input:** A protected route combining a quick historical view with the daily entry form.
